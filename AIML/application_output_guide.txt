This page has TWO main sections side by side:

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION: INPUT FORM — 13 Clinical Parameters
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Each field has: a label, an asterisk (*) meaning required, a tooltip on
hover, and an error message area below it.

FIELD 1: Age
  Type:       Numeric text entry
  Range:      29 to 120 years
  Tooltip:    "Patient's age in years (29–77 typical range)"
  Example:    55
  MEANING:    The patient's age. Older age increases heart disease risk.

FIELD 2: Sex
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "Female" (0), "Male" (1)
  Tooltip:    "0 = Female, 1 = Male"
  MEANING:    Biological sex. Males have statistically higher heart
              disease rates in the training dataset.

FIELD 3: Chest Pain Type (cp)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "Typical Angina" (0)      — Classic exertional chest pain
              "Atypical Angina" (1)     — Unusual chest pain pattern
              "Non-anginal Pain" (2)    — Chest pain not from the heart
              "Asymptomatic" (3)        — No chest pain symptoms
  Tooltip:    "0 = Typical Angina, 1 = Atypical, 2 = Non-anginal,
               3 = Asymptomatic"
  MEANING:    The type of chest pain the patient experiences.
              "Asymptomatic" (3) is actually the MOST concerning because
              it means heart disease may be present without obvious pain.

FIELD 4: Resting Blood Pressure (trestbps)
  Type:       Numeric text entry
  Range:      50 to 250 mm Hg
  Tooltip:    "Resting blood pressure in mm Hg (94–200 typical)"
  Unit:       mm Hg (millimeters of mercury)
  Example:    140
  MEANING:    Blood pressure measured while the patient is at rest.
              Normal: <120 mm Hg. High (hypertension): >140 mm Hg.
              High blood pressure is a major heart disease risk factor.

FIELD 5: Serum Cholesterol (chol)
  Type:       Numeric text entry
  Range:      50 to 700 mg/dl
  Tooltip:    "Serum cholesterol in mg/dl (126–564 typical)"
  Unit:       mg/dl (milligrams per deciliter)
  Example:    260
  MEANING:    Total cholesterol level in the blood.
              Normal: <200 mg/dl. Borderline: 200–239. High: ≥240.
              High cholesterol leads to plaque buildup in arteries.

FIELD 6: Fasting Blood Sugar (fbs)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "≤ 120 mg/dl (Normal)" (0)
              "> 120 mg/dl (High)" (1)
  Tooltip:    "Fasting blood sugar > 120 mg/dl: 0 = No, 1 = Yes"
  MEANING:    Whether blood sugar after fasting exceeds 120 mg/dl.
              High fasting blood sugar may indicate diabetes, which
              is a risk factor for heart disease.

FIELD 7: Resting ECG (restecg)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "Normal" (0)                    — Normal ECG reading
              "ST-T Abnormality" (1)          — ST-T wave changes
              "LV Hypertrophy" (2)            — Left ventricle thickening
  Tooltip:    "0 = Normal, 1 = ST-T abnormality, 2 = LV hypertrophy"
  MEANING:    Results of the resting electrocardiogram test.
              ECG = Electrocardiogram, a test that measures heart
              electrical activity. ST-T waves are specific patterns
              on the ECG trace. Abnormalities suggest heart problems.
              LV Hypertrophy = left ventricle wall thickening.

FIELD 8: Maximum Heart Rate (thalach)
  Type:       Numeric text entry
  Range:      50 to 250 bpm
  Tooltip:    "Maximum heart rate achieved (71–202 typical)"
  Unit:       bpm (beats per minute)
  Example:    145
  MEANING:    The highest heart rate the patient achieved during
              exercise testing. Lower maximum heart rates can indicate
              heart problems (the heart cannot work hard enough).

FIELD 9: Exercise Induced Angina (exang)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "No" (0), "Yes" (1)
  Tooltip:    "Exercise induced angina: 0 = No, 1 = Yes"
  MEANING:    Whether the patient experienced chest pain (angina)
              during exercise. "Yes" strongly suggests coronary
              artery disease because the heart cannot get enough
              blood during physical exertion.

FIELD 10: ST Depression / Oldpeak (oldpeak)
  Type:       Numeric text entry
  Range:      0.0 to 10.0
  Tooltip:    "ST depression induced by exercise (0.0–6.2 typical)"
  Unit:       Millimeters of ST segment depression
  Example:    2.0
  MEANING:    How much the ST segment on the ECG drops during exercise
              compared to rest. Higher values indicate more severe
              heart ischemia (lack of blood flow to the heart).
              ST = a specific segment between the S and T waves on ECG.

FIELD 11: ST Slope (slope)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "Upsloping" (0)     — ST segment goes upward (more normal)
              "Flat" (1)          — ST segment stays level (concerning)
              "Downsloping" (2)   — ST segment goes downward (most concerning)
  Tooltip:    "Slope of peak exercise ST: 0 = Up, 1 = Flat, 2 = Down"
  MEANING:    The direction of the ST segment slope during peak exercise.
              Downsloping is the most indicative of heart disease.

FIELD 12: Number of Major Vessels (ca)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "0" (0), "1" (1), "2" (2), "3" (3), "4" (4)
  Tooltip:    "Number of major vessels colored by fluoroscopy (0–4)"
  MEANING:    How many of the major coronary arteries showed blockage
              when viewed with fluoroscopy (a type of X-ray imaging
              using a contrast dye). More blocked vessels = higher risk.
              0 = no blocked vessels, 4 = all four major vessels blocked.

FIELD 13: Thalassemia (thal)
  Type:       Dropdown menu
  Values:     "Normal" (0)            — No thalassemia
              "Fixed Defect" (1)      — Permanent heart muscle damage
              "Reversible Defect" (2) — Temporary blood flow problem
              "Thalassemia" (3)       — Blood disorder present
  Tooltip:    "0 = Normal, 1 = Fixed defect, 2 = Reversible defect,
               3 = Thalassemia"
  MEANING:    Results from a thallium stress test. Thallium is a
              radioactive tracer injected into the blood to show
              blood flow through the heart muscle.
              Fixed defect = permanent damage (e.g., from a past
              heart attack). Reversible defect = temporary reduced
              blood flow (suggests treatable coronary artery disease).

Precision   0.8182    🎯    Blue     Of patients PREDICTED to have disease,

                                     Formula: 2 × (Precision × Recall) /

                                     Range: 0 to 1 (higher is better).
                                     Useful when you care about both false
                                     positives AND false negatives.

ROC AUC     0.8626    📐    Purple   Area Under the ROC Curve.
                                     Measures how well the model separates
                                     disease from no-disease across ALL
                                     probability thresholds.

                                     0.8626 = "good" discrimination ability.
                                     Advantage: Not affected by class imbalance.

SECTION: ROC CURVE (Chart)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OUTPUT ELEMENT               MEANING
───────────────────────────  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
ROC Curve (blue line)        Receiver Operating Characteristic curve.
                             Plots True Positive Rate (y-axis) vs
                             False Positive Rate (x-axis) at every
                             possible classification threshold.

AUC = 0.863 (shaded area)   Area Under the Curve. The total area below
                             the blue curve. Larger area = better model.
                             0.5 = useless (random), 1.0 = perfect.

Diagonal dashed line         Represents a random classifier (coin flip).
                             Any model should be ABOVE this line.

WHERE:
  True Positive Rate (TPR)  = Recall = TP / (TP + FN) = Sensitivity

INTERPRETATION:
  The curve bows toward the top-left corner, which is ideal.
  At any threshold, the model catches more true positives than
  it creates false positives. AUC of 0.863 is considered "good".

7. PAGE 4 — SHAP ANALYSIS
================================================================================

SHAP = SHapley Additive exPlanations

SHAP is based on Shapley values from cooperative game theory (1953).
It answers: "How much did each feature contribute to THIS specific
prediction, compared to the average prediction?"

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION: PLOT SELECTION BUTTONS (Top row)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BUTTON              ACTION
──────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Summary Plot        Shows beeswarm plot of SHAP values for ALL test patients
Bar Plot            Shows mean absolute SHAP values per feature
Waterfall Plot      Shows feature breakdown for a SINGLE patient prediction
Force Plot          Shows push/pull forces for a SINGLE prediction
Dependence Plot     Shows how ONE feature's value relates to its SHAP value
Patient SHAP        Shows SHAP values for YOUR most recent prediction input

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PLOT 1: SHAP SUMMARY PLOT (Beeswarm)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ELEMENT                 MEANING
──────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each dot                One patient (test sample)
X-axis                  SHAP value = impact on model output
                          Positive = pushes toward "Disease"
                          Negative = pushes toward "No Disease"
Y-axis                  Feature name (sorted by importance)
Dot color               Feature VALUE for that patient:
                          Red/Pink  = HIGH feature value
                          Blue      = LOW feature value
Dot position            How much that feature pushed the prediction

HOW TO READ EXAMPLE:
  If "Age" has red dots on the RIGHT side:
    → High age values push predictions TOWARD heart disease.
  If "Maximum Heart Rate" has red dots on the LEFT side:
    → High heart rates push predictions AWAY from heart disease.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PLOT 2: SHAP BAR PLOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ELEMENT                 MEANING
──────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Horizontal bars         Mean |SHAP value| for each feature across ALL patients
Bar length              Average absolute contribution to predictions
                          Longer bar = more important feature
Sorted order            Most important at the top

DIFFERENCE FROM FEATURE IMPORTANCE:
  Feature Importance (Performance page) = how often the feature is used
  SHAP Bar Plot = how much the feature CHANGES the output on average
  SHAP is more mathematically rigorous and theoretically grounded.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PLOT 3: SHAP WATERFALL PLOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ELEMENT                 MEANING
──────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
E[f(x)] or base value   The average model output (starting point)
Each bar                 One feature's contribution:
  Red bars               Push prediction HIGHER (toward disease)
  Blue bars              Push prediction LOWER (away from disease)
Final value f(x)         The actual model output for this patient
Arrow direction          Shows whether feature increased or decreased risk

HOW TO READ:
  Start at the base value. Each feature adds or subtracts from it.
  The final result is the predicted log-odds for this patient.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PLOT 4: SHAP FORCE PLOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ELEMENT                 MEANING
──────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Red region              Features pushing TOWARD heart disease
Blue region             Features pushing AWAY FROM heart disease
Width of each segment   Magnitude of that feature's contribution
Base value              Average prediction (where red and blue meet)
Bold value              The actual prediction output

This is a "tug of war" visualization — red and blue forces compete.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PLOT 5: SHAP DEPENDENCE PLOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ELEMENT                 MEANING
──────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
X-axis                  The selected feature's actual values
Y-axis                  The SHAP value for that feature
Each dot                One patient
Dot color               Value of the strongest INTERACTING feature

LIME = Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations

LIME explains individual predictions by creating a simpler,
interpretable "surrogate" model around the specific patient's data.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION: ACTION BUTTON
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Generate LIME Explanation" button (blue)
  - Requires a prediction to be run on the Patient Prediction page first
  - Shows a loading spinner during computation
  - LIME perturbs the input and trains a local linear model

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION: LIME BAR CHART
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ELEMENT                 MEANING
──────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Horizontal bars         Feature weights from the local linear model
Red bars                Features pushing toward "Heart Disease" (positive weight)
Blue bars               Features pushing toward "No Disease" (negative weight)
Bar length              Magnitude of contribution
Feature labels          May include conditions like "Age > 55.50" or
                        "Chest Pain Type <= 0.50" — these are the
                        discretized feature conditions LIME identified.
Sorted order            Features sorted by absolute weight (most impactful first)

LEGEND:
  Red  = "Pushes toward Disease"
  Blue = "Pushes toward No Disease"

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION: FEATURE CONTRIBUTION TABLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A tabular display with three columns:

COLUMN          MEANING
──────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Feature         The feature condition identified by LIME.
                Example: "Age > 55.50" means this patient's age is above 55.5
                Example: "0.50 < Thalassemia <= 2.50" means thal value is 1 or 2

Weight          The numerical weight (coefficient) from the local linear model.
                Example: +0.1234 or -0.0567
                Larger absolute value = stronger contribution.
                The sign indicates direction (positive = toward disease).

Direction       ▲ Positive = pushes prediction toward Heart Disease
                ▼ Negative = pushes prediction toward No Disease
                Color coded: Red for positive, Blue for negative.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION: LOCAL MODEL FIDELITY SCORE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OUTPUT                              MEANING
──────────────────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────
"Local Model Fidelity (R²): 0.85"  R-squared score of the local linear model.
                                    How well the simple linear model approximates
                                    the complex GradientBoosting model LOCALLY
                                    (in the neighborhood of this patient).

                                    Range: 0.0 to 1.0
                                    1.0 = perfect local approximation
                                    0.0 = linear model cannot explain the
                                          complex model's behavior here

                                    Values > 0.7 are generally good.
                                    Higher R² = more trustworthy explanation.

SECTION: PLAIN-ENGLISH EXPLANATION

Text panel explaining the LIME results in natural language.

   The model made this prediction based on the following
   local feature contributions:

     ✦ Age > 55.50: weight = +0.0891 (contradicts the prediction)
     ✧ 0.50 < Chest Pain Type <= 1.50: weight = -0.1234 (supports the prediction)

   Positive weights push the prediction toward Heart Disease.
   Negative weights push the prediction toward No Disease.

WHERE:

  ✧ (open diamond)   = Negative weight (pushes toward no disease)
  "supports"         = Agrees with the final prediction
  "contradicts"      = Disagrees with the final prediction

15. MODEL DETAILS
================================================================================

MODEL: GradientBoostingClassifier (from scikit-learn)

HYPERPARAMETER          VALUE       MEANING
──────────────────────  ──────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────
n_estimators            200         Maximum number of boosting stages (trees).
                                    Actual used: 19 (stopped early).
learning_rate           0.1         How much each new tree contributes.
                                    Smaller = more conservative, needs more trees.
max_depth               4           Maximum depth of each individual tree.
                                    Deeper = more complex patterns learned.
min_samples_split       5           Minimum samples needed to split a tree node.
min_samples_leaf        3           Minimum samples in each leaf node.
subsample               0.85        Fraction of data used per tree (stochastic).
                                    <1.0 adds randomness, reduces overfitting.
validation_fraction     0.15        15% of training data held for early stopping.
n_iter_no_change        15          Stop if no improvement for 15 consecutive rounds.
tol                     1e-4        Minimum improvement threshold (0.0001).
random_state            42          Random seed for reproducibility.

PREPROCESSING:
  StandardScaler — Transforms each feature to have mean=0 and std=1.
  This ensures features with large ranges (e.g., cholesterol 50–700)
  don't dominate over features with small ranges (e.g., sex 0–1).

TRAINING DATA:
  241 patients (80% of 302)
  Stratified split (preserves class balance in train and test)

TEST DATA:
  61 patients (20% of 302)
  Used for all evaluation metrics
